# Privacy policy: the halogenOS Group Assistant

This policy tells you what we keep about you, why we keep it, who receives it, and how long we keep it. We give this notice under Articles 13 and 14 GDPR.

## Definitions

- **halogenOS** is an open-source Android distribution.
- **The chat platform** is the service the Groups run on, currently Telegram (hereinafter "the chat platform"). It is a controller of its own under the GDPR and not our processor.
- **The chat groups** are the halogenOS community groups on the chat platform (hereinafter "the Groups", each "a Group").
- **The halogenOS Group Assistant** is the software we run as a member of the Groups (hereinafter "the Assistant").
- **A conversation** is the messages of a Group as we keep them.
- **The record** is what we keep about one item: a message, a join announcement, a report or a reaction.
- **Our processor** is the company that processes personal data for us on our instructions, under Article 28 GDPR.

## 2. What the Assistant does in a Group

The Assistant answers questions in a Group. It receives every message with text posted there, including the messages that never address it. When it answers, it uses the Group's conversation as the context around the question.

The Assistant checks the messages and the join announcements in a Group against the Group's rules. When it finds a clear violation, it reports that message or announcement with a public reply in the Group. A joining account whose displayed name is itself an advertisement is reported on the strength of its announcement, before the account has posted anything. The Group's administrators decide what happens next. The Assistant itself cannot warn, remove or ban anybody. The language model that writes the answers performs the check, and it can misfire and report a message that broke no rule. The Assistant can also put one emoji reaction on a message instead of answering it.

The Assistant is not available in private messages. We refuse a direct message and keep nothing from it. We do not look for sensitive content. Whatever a message carries, we keep its text and send it to the language model like any other text.

## 3. What we keep about you

We obtain your data from the chat platform and from the Groups. The Assistant produces the rest from what it receives. We obtain nothing about you from anywhere else. We keep no pictures or files, only text. We keep no post made anonymously for a Group and no message forwarded into it from a channel, except the Group's rules text.

We keep this data to answer questions in the Groups with their conversation as context, to attribute messages to accounts and to address you in an answer, to check the messages and announcements against the Group's rules, to act on abuse, and to limit how much the Assistant answers you. Your account ID also makes deletion possible. ## 4. Who receives your data

When the Assistant answers you, it sends the conversation to a language model, and our processor passes it along.

**What is sent:**

- our instructions to the model
- the Group's title and its rules
- the messages, with the username of each writer who has one
- the Assistant's earlier answers
- the summary, where the conversation continues from one
- with a join announcement, the name and username it showed, the announcement's message number and its send time
- with each message, its number on the chat platform and the time you sent it

**What is never sent:**

- your account ID
- the time a message reached us
- the standing we keep beside your message

A message we have already erased is sent only as a marker with no number. When the Assistant checks whether a username belongs to an administrator, the request also says whether that person was one.

Our processor is Requesty Ltd of London, United Kingdom. Requesty passes your conversation to a language model and returns the answer. Its processing sits in the EU: what it keeps, it keeps in Frankfurt. It stores no message and no answer and trains on neither, and it keeps billing records that carry no content.

We keep what we hold on a server in Germany. Handing your data to a United Kingdom company is a transfer outside the EU and the EEA. The European Commission's adequacy decision for the United Kingdom covers it, and no further safeguard is needed. The model answering your messages runs in the EU. If a model outside the EEA ever served a request, the standard contractual clauses, Article 46(2)(c) GDPR, would apply. You can write to privacy@halogenos.org for a copy of them.

The chat platform sits outside the EU and the EEA. It delivers every message and answer under its own privacy policy. It is where your data comes from, within the meaning of Article 14(2)(f) GDPR.

## 5. How long we keep it

We keep a conversation while it is in use, and we delete it whole once nothing has been added for 90 days. That means no message, no answer, and nothing the Assistant recorded. When a conversation grows long, the Assistant continues from a summary of its older part. We delete that older part 90 days after anything was last added to it. The summary is part of the conversation that continues, so it goes when that conversation goes. A message refreshes only the conversation it lands in.

Your username and your account ID go once the last thing naming you has gone. While an opt-out stands, both stay until you ask for deletion, and the table above says what that leaves.

A deletion request never waits for the 90 days: we act on it when it arrives. If the service stops for good, we delete everything with it.

## 6. How to stop or delete, and what stays afterward

You can stop collection or have your data deleted from inside a Group.

- The command /privacyout stops us collecting your messages from that moment, and answering any you send after it. We also keep no announcement that you joined while the opt-out stands.
- The command /privacydelete, confirmed with /confirmdelete within five minutes, removes what the table in section 3 marks as removed.
- The command /unblockprivacy turns collection back on.

You can also ask the Assistant in plain words to stop collecting. The commands are the sure way. After you opt out, the only messages of yours we keep are the privacy commands in this section and /privacy. Everything else is discarded on arrival. The commands act on the sending account immediately, on your own confirmed instruction.

For everything else, or whenever you prefer a person, write to [privacy@halogenos.org](mailto:privacy@halogenos.org). Tell us your username and the Group in that message. Because a username is visible to everyone, we may ask you to show that the request comes from that account. We ask for nothing more than that. We answer within one month, we charge nothing, and a person answers your email.

You can ask us to delete a message that concerns you but that you did not write. A person weighs your request against the writer's rights, decides, and tells you the outcome.

Asking us to delete removes nothing from the Group. Your messages stay where you posted them, under the chat platform's own rules. We cannot reach into the Group and take them down. Deleting a message on the chat platform does not by itself remove our copy, and neither your leaving a Group nor the Assistant leaving it deletes anything. Asking us is the route. When a Group administrator deletes a message with the Group's deletion command, we delete our copy of it with every version. The platform passes us no other deletion of a member's message. The Assistant can take back only the messages it sent itself, and only within the 48 hours the platform allows.

## 7. Your other rights, complaints, and changes

You can ask for confirmation and access under Article 15 GDPR. You can ask for rectification under Article 16, erasure under Article 17, and restriction under Article 18. Portability under Article 20 does not apply here. It covers data processed on consent or for a contract, and everything here rests on legitimate interest.

You can object at any time to any processing described here. Your reasons only need to come from your own situation. We then stop, unless we can show compelling legitimate reasons that override your interests, rights and freedoms. The same holds when we need the data for legal claims. In practice we grant every objection. You object with the /privacyout command or by writing to us, and neither asks you for a justification.
