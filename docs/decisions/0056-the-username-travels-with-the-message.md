# The username travels with the message

Date: 2026-08-23. The operator's decision.

The projection sends each chat message to the model provider together with its
sender's public username, so the assistant can mention a person in an answer
the way group members do. The username is the whole of it: the display name
and the platform's numeric account identifier stay on the machine, and the
identity tables keep owning who is who. The mention capability is the reason
and its bound — an answer that cannot address anyone is a worse group member,
and the handle is the smallest identifier that makes addressing work.

This supersedes the earlier posture that no identifier leaves the machine,
which the compliance documents carried until today. The documents move with
the decision: the policy states the transmission plainly, the assessments
carry the username as a transmitted identifier, and the record of processing
lists it as a data item that reaches the processor.

The projection change that makes it true follows as its own unit; until it
ships, the published policy over-describes the transmission, which is the
harmless direction — a policy may claim more processing than happens, never
less.

## Rejected

- **The pseudonymous per-conversation speaker label.** The earlier design:
  identity stays local, threading survives through a neutral label. It fails
  the product: the assistant cannot mention anyone, and a label leaking into
  an answer reads as gibberish in the group. The mention capability outranks
  the minimization step, by the operator's call.
- **Sending the display name or the numeric identifier too.** Neither adds
  addressing power the handle lacks, and both widen the transmitted identity
  for nothing.
